# Decide

Every screen poses a question. Name **the decision**, then design what helps the user answer it.

- Name the decision first. Write down what the user chooses here: which desk, which plan, which carrier. Judge every element by whether it helps that choice.
- Keep the decision's inputs together and in view. A screen that got its calm by moving filters onto separate pages makes users choose blind: two filters cost six taps, and each choice ignores the other. Choices that trade off (price against distance, speed against cost) share one surface, with the results visible as they change.
- Show the consequence before the commit. Each option states what it costs or yields; the action button states its result ("Show 23 desks"); counts or a distribution show where the options cluster.
- Ask for what the user knows, and compute the rest. Recognition beats recall:
  - recent recipients with photos, not an account number
  - monthly budget and loan term, from which you compute the car price they can afford
  - the common answers as taps, plus "Other"
- Turn hard questions into easy ones. "Which plan suits my team?" is hard; "Your 4 seats cost $36 on Team, $48 on Pro" answers it. Give one number per option. People read a range at its high end and carry that doubt to every option.
- Answer the blocking doubt at the button. The worry that stalls the choice (can I cancel, are there fees, will it fit) gets a one-line answer right under the call to action.
- Be specific. Say "Arrives Thursday", "4 desks free", and "Ready in 2 taps" instead of "soon", "available", and "easy".
- Fit the control to how the user knows the value: nudging a value they have, picking from a known set, exploring a range, or typing a precise number. Values: `references/components.md` (Input choice).
- Offer a switch when no default fits everyone, such as a grid for browsing and a compact list for comparing.
- Adapt home to the user's stage. New: setup and a few starting points. Returning: today's work. Expert: their numbers first.
- Make forms ask little and help a lot. Visible labels above fields, sensible defaults, validation on blur, an error under the field that says how to fix it, and the user's input kept on error. Values: `references/components.md` (Forms).

**Check:** Can you name the decision on each screen? Is each option's consequence visible before the commit? Does any answer depend on information that lives on another screen?
