# Opinion

Every element earns its place with a reason the user would give. When you borrow a pattern, borrow it for the reason it exists, never for its look alone.

- Before the first pixel, write a brief that answers four questions: why someone opens this screen, what they do in it on a normal day, where they go next, and what you will deliberately leave out.
- Test every borrowed part against its reason. Name the problem the pattern solved in its original product, then check that the problem exists here. A workspace name in the account menu helps people who belong to several workspaces; in a single-account tool it's noise. A convention that has become generic (a chevron meaning "opens a menu") travels freely.
- Pick, and accept that someone will disagree. A screen without a point of view converges on the median generated UI: centered hero, gradient, three cards. Make each choice one you could defend in a sentence.
- Ship fewer things, and finish them. Every feature, option, and control has to pay for its place; three that work completely beat ten that half work.
- Treat copy as design. Buttons say verb + object ("Export invoices"), errors say what happened and what to do next, and headings say what the user gets. Values: `references/type.md` (Copy).

**Check:** For each element, can you state its reason in one line, from the user's side? For each borrowed pattern, does its original reason hold here?
